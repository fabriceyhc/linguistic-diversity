# Human Agreement, and What Temperature Actually Moves

Two questions on the three human-scored datasets that ship with the McDiv download
(Tevet & Berant, [EACL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02990)). Only one of the three
had ever been used here, which left every tuning decision resting on a single corpus.

```bash
# data lives in ../embedder_selection/data (see that README for the download)
python run_study.py
```

| dataset | sets | responses each | labelled by |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `McDiv_nuggets` | 600 | 5 | content diversity |
| `conTest` | 670 | 5 | content diversity |
| `decTest` | 609 | 10 | **sampling temperature** |
| `decTest` (unscored) | 2,979 | 10 | sampling temperature |

## 1. Agreement generalises — and the pattern is the point

Spearman ρ against graded human diversity scores.

| Metric | claims | McDiv_nuggets | conTest | decTest |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `DocumentSemantics` | semantic | **+0.581** | **+0.650** | +0.778 |
| `SelfBLEU` | *(form baseline)* | +0.393 | +0.538 | **+0.818** |
| `TypeTokenRatio` | *(form baseline)* | +0.379 | +0.514 | +0.817 |
| `DistinctN` | *(form baseline)* | +0.379 | +0.514 | +0.817 |
| `TokenSemantics` | semantic | +0.388 | +0.304 | +0.551 |
| `Phonemic` | phonological | +0.213 | +0.368 | +0.633 |
| `DependencyParse` | syntactic | +0.208 | +0.081 | +0.593 |
| `PartOfSpeechSequence` | morphological | +0.192 | +0.296 | +0.689 |
| `Rhythmic` | phonological | +0.151 | +0.299 | +0.583 |

`DocumentSemantics` leads on both **content-diversity** datasets, and the ordering there is
the discriminant result restated on human data: the metrics that do not claim to measure
content — syntax, morphology, phonology — agree weakly with humans rating content, exactly
as they should. `DependencyParse` at +0.081 on conTest is the extreme case, and it is a
pass, not a failure. A syntactic metric that tracked content judgments closely would be
measuring the wrong thing.

**On `decTest` the form baselines win** (+0.818 against +0.778). That is not a defeat
either: `decTest` varies by sampling temperature, temperature moves form most (below), so
the diversity humans are responding to there *is* largely form. It does mean a single
headline agreement number is meaningless without saying what the dataset varies.

## 2. What sampling temperature moves

Tevet & Berant report that decoding parameters "mostly affect form but not meaning". They
showed it with one content metric and one form metric. `decTest` carries the temperature
that generated each set, so the claim can be measured at every level at once — 2,979 sets,
temperature 0.21 to 1.21.

| Metric | level | ρ vs temperature |
|---|---|---:|
| `TypeTokenRatio` | lexical (form) | **+0.724** |
| `DistinctN` | lexical (form) | **+0.724** |
| `SelfBLEU` | lexical (form) | +0.663 |
| `PartOfSpeechSequence` | morphological | +0.607 |
| `DependencyParse` | syntactic | +0.582 |
| `Phonemic` | phonological | +0.568 |
| `DocumentSemantics` | semantic | +0.539 |
| `Rhythmic` | phonological | +0.535 |
| `TokenSemantics` | semantic | **+0.224** |

The ordering is clean and it is the shape Tevet & Berant predicted: **form (0.66–0.72) >
structure (0.57–0.61) > content (0.22–0.54)**. Turning up temperature buys you different
words far more readily than different things to say.

Two qualifications the ordering alone hides:

- **It is a gradient, not a dichotomy.** `DocumentSemantics` at +0.539 is not flat.
  Temperature does move content, just less than it moves surface form. The claim is
  quantitative and this is the quantity.
- **`TokenSemantics` at +0.224 is the strongest form of the result** — the metric that
  moves least is the one measuring contextual meaning token by token.

The practical reading: if you are tuning temperature to make a system's outputs more
diverse, and you measure with distinct-*n* or Self-BLEU, you will see a large improvement
that is mostly rewording. Measure content separately or the knob will flatter itself.

## Caveats

- Three datasets, one generation setup, English only. The ordering is consistent across
  all three but they share provenance.
- `decTest`'s human scores and its temperature label are not independent — the sets were
  generated by varying temperature — so §1's `decTest` column and §2 are two views of one
  manipulation, not separate evidence.
- Human scores are `metric_abs_hds_mean`, the mean absolute human diversity score shipped
  with the dataset. No re-annotation was done.
